Players bend their lamps' beams with shared mirrors to hold the reef's crystals alone. 2 to 4 players.

This is a league based challenge.

For this challenge, multiple leagues for the same game are available. Once a program has proven its worth against the Boss, it accesses the higher league.

This league: rocks, turning mirrors with locks, contested crystals and streaks.

 Goal

Score more points than the other players by lighting crystals with the beam of the player's lamp.

 Rules

The reef

The reef is represented as a square grid. The x-coordinate indicates the column (0 at the left), and the y-coordinate indicates the row (0 at the top/north). Its side is size cells.

2 to 4 players play. Each player owns a lamp on one edge. The reef looks the same from every lamp (90-degree rotational symmetry). With 2 players the lamps are on opposite edges; with 3 players one edge has no lamp.

Lamps on the edges, their 5 x 3 yards and the unobstructed straight path of each beam at the start; x grows to the east, y to the south.

Cells

Each cell of the reef is one of:

  • . empty: a beam goes through;
  • 1 a crystal: a beam goes through and lights the crystal;
  • A B C D a lamp: a beam that reaches it stops;
  • # rock: a beam stops.
  • Every crystal is worth 1.

Lamps and beams

A lamp fires a straight-line beam towards the opposite edge.

  • Every lamp fires one beam each turn, after all actions of the turn.
  • The beam moves cell by cell until it leaves the reef, reaches a rock or reaches a lamp.
  • A beam that reaches its own lamp stops there and scores the crystals it lit on the way.
  • Beams never block each other.
  • A beam cannot loop: it never enters the same cell twice in the same direction.
  • A beam that crosses the same crystal twice lights it once.
  • At the start, the straight line of each lamp holds no crystal, rock or mirror: a beam must be bent to score.
A beam stops when it leaves the reef, reaches a rock or reaches a lamp.

Mirrors

A mirror turns a beam by 90 degrees:

MirrorBeam moving eastnorthwestsouth
type 0 = /leaves northleaves eastleaves southleaves west
type 1 = \leaves southleaves westleaves northleaves east

Mirrors belong to nobody: a mirror bends every beam that reaches it. Players cannot remove mirrors. Mirrors stay until the end.

The reef starts with a few mirrors. They appear in the mirror list from turn 1; the reef rows show their cells as ..

Mirror supply

  • Each player starts with 12 mirrors available for placement.
  • At the start of every 10th turn, beginning with turn 10, each player gets 1 more mirror. The additional mirror is already included in the turn's input.
  • The number of available mirrors is not capped.
  • A shattered mirror is permanently lost and is not returned to the player.

Placement restrictions

Yards.

  • Each lamp's yard is the 5 x 3 block of cells around it (the lamp cell, and every cell up to 2 cells away along the edge and up to 2 cells deep, diagonals included).
  • Only the yard's owner may place or turn mirrors there; on every other cell any player may place or turn mirrors (mirrors have no owner).
  • Beams cross yards freely.
  • With 3 players the edge without a lamp has no yard.
  • Lamps are not always at the middle of their edge: read their cells from the reef.
  • Every lamp is at least 2 cells from a corner, so its yard fits inside the reef.

Same cell.

  • If two or more players place a mirror on the same cell in the same turn, all of them fail and keep their mirror.
  • If several players turn the same mirror in the same turn, it flips once.

Actions

Each turn, each player outputs one action:

  • PLACE x y type: put one of the player's available mirrors on an empty cell (see Mirror supply).
  • TURN x y: flip a mirror (/ becomes \ and back).
  • WAIT: do nothing.

Locks. Each mirror has a lock. A mirror can be turned only when its lock is 0. The lock is set to 2 when the mirror becomes active or is turned, then drops by 1 at the end of each following turn (so it reads 2 on the next turn's input). A TURN is allowed from the first turn whose input shows lock 0: turn t+3 for a mirror placed on turn t. A mirror becomes active as soon as it is placed.

After a TURN the lock reads 2, then drops by 1 each turn; TURN works again at 0.

Impossible actions are replaced by WAIT (the reason is shown in the game summary):

  • a cell outside the reef
  • a type other than 0 or 1
  • a cell that is not empty (crystal, lamp, mirror, rock)
  • a cell in another player's yard
  • no available mirror
  • a number out of range (negative or too large)
  • turning where there is no active mirror or the lock is not 0

Invalid lines eliminate the player:

  • an empty line
  • an unknown keyword (keywords are in capitals)
  • missing numbers
  • a number that is not a whole number

Spaces at the start and end of the line are ignored.

Scoring

Each player has a streak (0 to 4) on each crystal. Each turn, for each crystal:

  • lit by exactly one player: that player's streak grows by 1 (max 4), then the player gains value times the new streak; every other player's streak drops by 1 (min 0);
  • lit by two or more players: nobody gains; those players keep their streak; the others' drop by 1;
  • lit by nobody: every streak drops by 1 (min 0).

A crystal kept alone pays x1, x2, x3, then x4 every turn.

Order of a turn

  • Mirror supply (at the start of every 10th turn)
  • All placements, then all turns.
  • Every lamp fires.
  • Scoring.
  • Locks drop by 1, except those set on this turn.

End of the game

The game lasts 150 turns, or ends earlier when:

  • fewer than two players remain;
  • every remaining player has done nothing (WAIT, an impossible action, or a placement that failed on a shared cell) for 20 turns in a row: the referee then plays the remaining turns itself with every player waiting (lamps still fire and score each turn), and the final scores are the scores after the last turn.

Equal scores share the rank. An eliminated player gets a score of -1. Its lamp stops firing and acts as a rock; its yard stays protected; its mirrors stay on the reef. The input shows it with -1 mirrors.

Victory Conditions
  • Have the highest score when the game ends.
Defeat Conditions
  • Have a lower score than another player when the game ends.
  • The program does not answer in time, crashes, or outputs a line that is not a valid command (keyword and whole numbers): the player is eliminated.

Example (an example reef, not a real map)

size = 13. A lamp at (0,3) fires east; a crystal of value 1 is at (5,6). Its owner outputs PLACE 5 3 1. The beam goes (1,3) ... (5,3), turns south on \, crosses (5,4), (5,5), lights (5,6) and goes on south. If no other beam lights it, the player gains 1, 2, 3, then 4 per turn.


 Technical Details

  • Rows and lists are sorted by y, then x.
  • Derived information: the turn number is the count of turns read (the first is 1); the other players' scores and streaks follow from the beams, which can be traced each turn from the mirror list of the next turn.
  • The referee source code is on GitHub
  • Glintwatch is an original game; its idea comes from the genre of laser-and-mirror reflection puzzles.

 Game Input/Output

Player ids

Players are numbered 0 to number of players - 1. In the input, the receiving player is always player 0; the other players follow in clockwise order of the lamps around the reef, starting after player 0's lamp. Unused ids show -1 mirrors.

The lamp letters follow these ids: A is player 0's lamp. The reef and the coordinates are the same for every player (not rotated); only the lamp letters change.

Initialization input

Line 1: an integer playerCount: the number of players (2 to 4), also the number of lamps on the reef.

Line 2: an integer size: the width and height of the reef.

Next size lines: a string row of size characters, the reef rows from y = 0: character x of row y is cell (x, y). . empty, # rock, 1 crystal, A B C D lamp of player 0 1 2 3.

Input for one game turn

A field that this league does not use has a fixed value, stated below. Turns are numbered from 1; the input is not numbered.

Line 1: five space-separated integers myScore mirrors0 mirrors1 mirrors2 mirrors3:

  • myScore: the score of player 0.
  • mirrors0 mirrors1 mirrors2 mirrors3: the mirrors that player 0, 1, 2, 3 can still place (-1 for an eliminated or missing player).

Line 2: an integer mirrorCount: the number of mirrors on the reef.

Next mirrorCount lines: five space-separated integers x y mirrorType lock life. Every listed mirror fills its cell: no mirror can be placed there.

  • x y: integers, the mirror's cell.
  • mirrorType: an integer, 0 (/) or 1 (\). The type is always real, pending or not.
  • lock: an integer, the number of turns before it can be turned; 0 when it can be turned.
  • life: an integer. Always -1: mirrors never wear in this league.

Next line: an integer crystalCount: the number of crystals.

Next crystalCount lines, one per crystal in reef order (by y, then x): three space-separated integers tide litBy myStreak:

  • tide: an integer, always 0 (unused; crystals are always awake).
  • litBy: an integer, the id of the player who lit it alone on the last turn; -1 nobody (and on turn 1); -2 several players.
  • myStreak: an integer, player 0's streak on it, 0 to 4.
Output for one game turn

One line: one action, optionally followed by a space and a message:

  • PLACE x y type: place a mirror on cell (x, y); type is 0 (/) or 1 (\).
  • TURN x y: flip the mirror on cell (x, y).
  • WAIT: do nothing.

Variables:

  • x y: integers, the column and the row of the cell.
  • type: an integer, 0 (/) or 1 (\).
  • message: an optional string of up to 30 characters: every token after the numbers. A longer message is cut, not refused.

Tokens are separated by spaces; any other separator (a tab, for example) makes the line invalid.

Example: PLACE 5 3 1 hello

Constraints
2 ≤ playerCount ≤ 4
13 ≤ size ≤ 19, size is odd
Response time for the first turn ≤ 1000 ms
Response time for one turn ≤ 50 ms
