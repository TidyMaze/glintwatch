Players bend their lamps' beams with shared mirrors to hold the reef's crystals alone. 2 to 4 players.

This is a league based challenge.

For this challenge, multiple leagues for the same game are available. Once a program has proven its worth against the Boss, it accesses the higher league.

This league: crystal tides, crystal values from 1 to 3, and strikes on rival lamps.

 Goal

Score more points than the other players by lighting crystals with the beam of the player's lamp.

 Rules

The reef

The reef is a square grid of size x size cells (size is odd, 13 to 19). x is the column (0 on the left), y is the row (0 at the top); north is towards y = 0. 2 to 4 players play. Each player owns a lamp on one edge. The reef looks the same from every lamp (90-degree rotational symmetry). With 2 players the lamps are on opposite edges; with 3 players one edge has no lamp.

Lamps on the edges, their 5 x 3 yards and empty lamp lines; x grows to the east, y to the south.

Cells:

  • . empty: a beam goes through;
  • 1 2 3 a crystal and its value: a beam goes through and lights the crystal;
  • A B C D a lamp: a beam that reaches it stops;
  • # rock: a beam stops.

Beams

After all actions of a turn, every lamp fires one beam. A lamp on the left edge fires east, on the top edge south, on the right edge west, on the bottom edge north. The beam moves cell by cell until it leaves the reef, reaches a rock or reaches a lamp. Beams never block each other. A beam cannot loop: it never enters the same cell twice in the same direction. A beam that crosses the same crystal twice lights it once. At the start, the straight line of each lamp holds no crystal, rock or mirror: a beam must be bent to score.

A beam stops when it leaves the reef, reaches a rock or reaches a lamp.

Mirrors

A mirror turns a beam by 90 degrees:

MirrorBeam moving eastnorthwestsouth
type 0 = /leaves northleaves eastleaves southleaves west
type 1 = \leaves southleaves westleaves northleaves east

Mirrors belong to nobody: a mirror bends every beam that reaches it. Players cannot remove mirrors. Mirrors wear out (see below).

The reef starts with a few mirrors. They appear in the mirror list from turn 1; the reef string shows their cells as ..

Actions

Each turn, each player prints one action:

  • PLACE x y type: put one of the player's mirrors on an empty cell.
  • TURN x y: flip a mirror (/ becomes \ and back).
  • WAIT: do nothing.

A message may follow the action, for example PLACE 5 3 1 hello (30 characters max).

Yards. Each lamp's yard is the 5 x 3 block of cells around it (the lamp cell, and every cell up to 2 cells away along the edge and up to 2 cells deep, diagonals included). Only the yard's owner may place or turn mirrors there; on every other cell any player may place or turn mirrors (mirrors have no owner). Beams cross yards freely. With 3 players the edge without a lamp has no yard. Lamps are not always at the middle of their edge: read their cells from the reef. Every lamp is at least 2 cells from a corner, so its yard fits inside the reef.

Same cell. If two or more players place a mirror on the same cell in the same turn, all of them fail and keep their mirror. If several players turn the same mirror in the same turn, it flips once.

Locks. Each mirror has a lock. A mirror can be turned only when its lock is 0. The lock is set to 5 when the mirror becomes active or is turned, then drops by 1 at the end of each following turn (so it reads 5 on the next turn's input). A TURN is allowed from the first turn whose input shows lock 0: turn t+7 for a mirror placed on turn t.

After a TURN the lock reads 5, then drops by 1 each turn; TURN works again at 0.

Impossible actions are replaced by WAIT (the reason is shown in the game summary): a cell outside the reef, a type other than 0 or 1, a cell that is not empty (rock, crystal, lamp, mirror, pending mirror), a cell in another player's yard, no mirror in hand, a number out of range (negative or too large), turning where there is no active mirror or the lock is not 0.

Invalid lines eliminate the player: an empty line, an unknown keyword (keywords are in capitals), missing numbers, or a number that is not a whole number. Spaces at the start and end of the line are ignored. Every token after the numbers is the message; a message longer than 30 characters is cut, not refused.

Scoring

Each player has a streak (0 to 4) on each crystal. Each turn, for each crystal:

  • if the crystal is asleep: all streaks on it become 0 and nobody gains;
  • lit by exactly one player: that player's streak grows by 1 (max 4), then the player gains value x the new streak; every other player's streak drops by 1 (min 0);
  • lit by two or more players: nobody gains; those players keep their streak; the others' drop by 1;
  • lit by nobody: every streak drops by 1 (min 0).

A crystal kept alone pays x1, x2, x3, then x4 every turn.

Mirror economy

  • Supply. Each player starts with 4 mirrors and gets 1 more at the start of turns 4, 8, 12 ... (the input of that turn already counts it). A player holds at most 6 mirrors in hand; extra mirrors are lost. A shattered mirror does not come back to any hand.
  • Build time. A mirror placed on turn t is pending on turn t+1 and bends beams from turn t+2. While pending, beams go through its cell and nobody can place there or turn it; the other players read its type as -1 (the player who placed it reads the real type). A pending mirror shows lock -1 and life 20.
  • Wear. life drops by 1 at the end of each turn on which the mirror bends beams, except a turn on which it is turned; at 0 the mirror shatters and its cell becomes empty. Timeline of a mirror placed on turn t and never turned: input t+1 shows it pending with life 20; inputs t+2 to t+21 show life 20 to 1 and it bends beams on those turns; it shatters at the end of turn t+21; input t+22 lists it once with life 0. Mirrors on the reef at the start show life 20 on turn 1.
Placed on turn t, pending on turn t+1, bends beams from turn t+2.
An active mirror bends beams for 20 turns, then shatters.

Tides, values and strikes

  • Tides. Each crystal is awake 30 turns out of every 45, then asleep 15 turns. The input gives, for each crystal, whether it is awake and in how many turns this changes. An asleep crystal gives nothing and resets the streaks on it.
  • Values. Crystals are worth 1, 2 or 3.
  • Strike. Each turn, a beam that ends on another player's lamp gives 2 points to the player who fired it. Each such beam counts. The struck player loses nothing. An eliminated player's lamp is a rock and gives nothing.
Values 1 to 3 by size; a crystal is awake 30 turns, then asleep 15 and pays nothing.
A beam that ends on another player's lamp: 2 points each turn.

Order of a turn

  • Supply (before the input is sent).
  • All placements, then all turns.
  • Every lamp fires.
  • Scoring.
  • Locks drop by 1, except those set on this turn.
  • Pending mirrors become active, active mirrors wear.
Supply, placements, turns, lamps fire, scoring, locks drop, mirrors activate and wear.

End of the game

The game ends after 150 turns, or earlier when:

  • fewer than two players remain;
  • the ranking can no longer change: every two remaining players' scores differ by more than the most points one player can still score (turns left after this one x (4 x the total value of all crystals + 2));
  • every remaining player has done nothing (WAIT, an impossible action, or a placement that failed on a shared cell) for 20 turns in a row: the referee then plays the remaining turns itself with every player waiting (lamps still fire and score each turn), and the final scores are the scores after the last turn.

Equal scores share the rank. An eliminated player gets a score of -1. Its lamp stops firing and acts as a rock; its yard stays protected; its mirrors stay on the reef. The input shows it with -1 mirrors.

Victory Conditions
  • Have the highest score when the game ends.
Defeat Conditions
  • Have a lower score than another player when the game ends.
  • The program does not answer in time, crashes, or prints a line that is not a valid command (keyword and whole numbers): the player is eliminated.

Example (an example reef, not a real map)

size = 13. A lamp at (0,3) fires east; a crystal of value 1 is at (5,6). Its owner prints PLACE 5 3 1. The beam goes (1,3) ... (5,3), turns south on \, crosses (5,4), (5,5), lights (5,6) and goes on south. If no other beam lights it, the player gains 1, 2, 3, then 4 per turn. The mirror starts bending beams 2 turns after it is placed.


 Technical Details

  • Rows and lists are sorted by y, then x.
  • Derived information: the turn number is the count of turns read (the first is 1); the number of players is the number of lamp letters in reef; the other players' scores and streaks follow from the beams, which can be traced each turn from the mirror list of the next turn.
  • The referee source code is on GitHub
  • Glintwatch is an original game; its idea comes from the genre of laser-and-mirror reflection puzzles.

 Game Input/Output

Ids. In the input, the receiving player is always player 0; the other players are 1, 2, 3 in clockwise order of the lamps around the reef (left edge, top edge, right edge, bottom edge), starting after player 0's lamp. With 2 players the other player is 1; with 3 players they are 1 and 2. Unused ids show -1 mirrors. The lamp letters follow these ids: A is player 0's lamp. The reef and the coordinates are the same for every player (not rotated); only the lamp letters change.

Initialization Input

Line 1: a string reef of size x size characters, the reef row by row from y = 0: cell (x, y) is character y x size + x. . empty, # rock, 1 2 3 crystal of that value, A B C D lamp of player 0 1 2 3. The number of lamps is the number of players (2 to 4).

Line 2: an integer size: the width and height of the reef.

Input for one game turn

Turns are numbered from 1; the input is not numbered.

Line 1: five space-separated integers myScore mirrors0 mirrors1 mirrors2 mirrors3: the score of player 0, then the mirrors each player id can still place (-1 for an eliminated or missing player).

Line 2: an integer mirrorCount: the number of mirrors on the reef.

Next mirrorCount lines: five space-separated integers x y mirrorType lock life. Every listed mirror, pending or active, fills its cell: no mirror can be placed there, except a mirror with life 0.

  • x y: the mirror's cell.
  • mirrorType: 0 (/) or 1 (\); -1 for another player's pending mirror.
  • lock: turns before it can be turned; 0 when it can be turned; -1 while it is pending (it does not bend beams yet).
  • life: active turns left before it shatters; 0 for a mirror that shattered at the end of the last turn (listed once; its cell is empty).

Next line: an integer crystalCount: the number of crystals.

Next crystalCount lines, one per crystal in reef order (by y, then x): three space-separated integers tide litBy myStreak:

  • tide: the state on this turn: k > 0 means awake on this turn and the next k - 1 turns; -k means asleep on this turn and the next k - 1 turns; never 0 in this league (tide 1 is followed by -15, and -1 by 30).
  • litBy: the id of the player who lit it alone on the last turn; -1 nobody (and on turn 1); -2 several players; it also reports beams on an asleep crystal.
  • myStreak: player 0's streak on it, 0 to 4.
Output for one game turn

A single line with one action, optionally followed by a space and a message (30 characters max):

  • PLACE x y type: place a mirror on cell (x, y); type is 0 (/) or 1 (\).
  • TURN x y: flip the mirror on cell (x, y).
  • WAIT: do nothing.

Tokens are separated by spaces; any other separator (a tab, for example) makes the line invalid.

Example: PLACE 5 3 1 hello

Constraints
2 ≤ number of players ≤ 4
13 ≤ size ≤ 19, size is odd
Response time for the first turn ≤ 1000 ms
Response time for one turn ≤ 50 ms
